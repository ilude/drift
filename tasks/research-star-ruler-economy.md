## Star Ruler 1 — Economy Overview

Star Ruler 1 (2010, Blind Mind Studios) uses a **stockpile-based production chain economy** with instant construction. Resources are physical quantities that accumulate in storage. If you have enough stockpiled, you can build anything instantly — there is no build queue timer. If you lack resources, construction pulls from the empire-wide **Galactic Bank** at a rate limited by your spaceport capacity.

The fundamental tension: production is rate-based (resources/tick), but consumption is instant (build costs). This makes stockpile management and throughput (spaceport bandwidth) the core economic constraints.

---

## Star Ruler 1 — Resource Model

### Core Production Chain

```
Ore (finite, mined from planets)
 └─► Metal (via Metal Mining Complex)
      ├─► Electronics (2 Metal → 1 Electronics, via Electronics Factory)
      │    └─► Advanced Parts (2 Metal + 1 Electronics → 1 Advanced Parts)
      └─► [direct use in construction]
```

### Resource Types

| Resource | Source | Role |
|----------|--------|------|
| **Ore** | Mined from planets (finite deposit) | Raw input to entire chain |
| **Metal** | Refined from Ore | Base construction material |
| **Electronics** | 2 Metal → 1 Electronics | Intermediate component |
| **Advanced Parts** | 2 Metal + 1 Electronics → 1 Adv. Parts | High-tech component |
| **Labor** | Generated by cities/shipyards | Required for all construction |
| **Food** | Farms | Prevents starvation/population decline |
| **Goods** | Goods Factories | Prevents unhappiness (unhappy pop = slower production) |
| **Luxuries** | Luxury Factories | Boosts happiness (happy pop = faster production) |

### Key Properties

- **Ore is finite but economy is not.** When ore depletes, Metal Mining Complexes still produce metal, just at a reduced rate. You need more complexes to compensate. Electronics and Advanced Parts remain infinite.
- **Conversion ratios matter.** 2:1 Metal→Electronics and (2+1):1 Metal+Electronics→Advanced Parts means the production chain needs careful balancing of factory counts.
- **Population happiness affects production rates.** Goods prevent penalties; Luxuries grant bonuses. Enslavement bypasses happiness but has other costs.
- **Ship-based economy.** Ships can mount economic subsystems: Metal Refinery, Electronics Fabricator, Advanced Parts Assembler, Export Dock. Mobile shipyards are possible.

## Star Ruler 1 — Colony Management

### Planet Slots

Planets have a fixed number of **building slots** determined by planet size. Larger planets can hold more structures. Typical range: small planets ~8-16 slots, large planets 25-30+ slots.

### Buildings (Planet Structures)

Key building types include:
- **Metal Mining Complex** — Refines ore into metal
- **Electronics Factory** — Converts metal into electronics
- **Advanced Parts Factory** — Converts metal + electronics into advanced parts
- **Farm** — Produces food
- **Goods Factory** — Produces goods for population happiness
- **Luxury Factory** — Produces luxury goods
- **City** — Houses population, provides labor
- **Spaceport** — Enables import/export from the Galactic Bank (throughput bottleneck)
- **Research Lab** — Generates research points
- **Shipyard** — Provides labor for ship construction

### Colony Ship Mechanics

Colony ships must be size 15+ to colonize. For each +15 size over the minimum, you get 1 extra starting building on the new colony. Larger colony ships = faster colony bootstrapping.

### Population

- Population grows naturally when fed.
- Population generates labor (needed for construction).
- Happiness (from Goods/Luxuries) modifies production efficiency.
- Starvation from food shortage causes population decline.

### Governor System

Players can assign automated governors to planets with preset specialization profiles:
- Food planet
- Metal factory
- Research focus
- Economic/construction hub

Governors auto-build appropriate structures, reducing micromanagement for large empires.

## Star Ruler 1 — Logistics & Trade

### The Galactic Bank

The **Galactic Bank** is a shared, empire-wide resource pool. Any planet with a Spaceport can:
- **Export** surplus resources to the Bank
- **Import** needed resources from the Bank

The rate of transfer is limited by **spaceport capacity** — more spaceports = faster import/export. This is the primary throughput bottleneck.

### Blockades

If enemies outnumber your forces 2:1 in a system, all planets and ships in that system lose Galactic Bank access. This makes system defense economically critical.

### Haulers

Hauler ships carry cargo within a system. Uses:
- **Bootstrap new colonies** — Send haulers alongside colony ships to feed resources to new planets before they have spaceports
- **Intra-system transfer** — Move resources between planets in the same system
- **Blockade resilience** — Distribute stored resources within a blockaded system
- **Late-game construction feeding** — Supply orbital construction stations from nearby planets

Haulers are most useful during colony establishment and become less critical once spaceports are built (spaceport → Galactic Bank is more efficient for cross-system transfer).

### Trade Docks & Refineries (Orbital)

- **Trade Docks** — Take resources from a planet and put them into the Galactic Bank
- **Refineries** — Take raw Ore from a planet and refine it to Metal/Electronics/Advanced Parts, freeing the planet to focus on mining

### Stockpiling Protocol

A civil act that makes spaceports maintain 95% storage levels instead of the default 50%, ensuring planets are always ready for instant construction.

## Star Ruler 1 — Economic Scaling

### Early Game (1-2 planets)

- Single homeworld produces everything
- Ore is plentiful, factory ratios don't matter much
- Labor is the main bottleneck (need cities)
- No spaceport network yet

### Mid Game (5-20 planets)

- Specialize planets: food worlds, factory worlds, research worlds
- Galactic Bank becomes the central distribution mechanism
- Spaceport count becomes the throughput bottleneck
- Ore starts depleting on early colonies; need more Metal Mining Complexes
- Governor system helps manage growing empire

### Late Game (50+ planets)

- Pure spaceport planets act as high-bandwidth import hubs feeding shipyards
- Orbital construction stations enable building in systems with many production planets
- Level 10 advanced stations produce ~50k metal/tick from a single planet, plus 10-15k electronics and advanced parts each
- Ships can be the size of stars; Ring Worlds become viable
- "Mega Construction" tech reduces cost of enormous ships
- Economy becomes about maximizing **throughput** rather than raw production

### Key Scaling Bottlenecks

1. **Spaceport bandwidth** — Can't build faster than you can import
2. **Ore depletion** — Requires more complexes for same output
3. **Factory ratios** — 2:1 and 3:1 conversion ratios mean you need careful balancing
4. **Planet slots** — Physical limit on buildings per planet
5. **Labor** — Need sufficient cities/population for construction

## Star Ruler 2 — Economy Overview

Star Ruler 2 (2015) **completely redesigned** the economy. Gone is the stockpile/production chain model. In its place: a **rate-based, pressure-driven, budget-cycle economy** with resource tiers and planet leveling.

The core philosophy shift: SR1 was about managing stockpiles and throughput; SR2 is about managing **resource networks** (which planets export what to where) and **pressure optimization** (maximizing income per pressure point).

The economy avoids a "Command & Conquer" direct-control model — most buildings are auto-built by civilians in response to economic pressure. The player's role is architect of the resource network, not micromanager of individual buildings.

## Star Ruler 2 — Resource Model

### Resource Tiers

Every planet has **one native resource** (determined at galaxy generation). Resources are tiered:

#### Tier 0 — Basic Resources (exportable from Level 0 planets)

**Water:**
| Resource | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| Water | +1 Water |
| Heavy Water | +1 Water, +3 random pressure |

**Food:**
| Resource | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| Meat, Fish, Fruit, Grain | +1 Food |
| Nillin Wheat | +2 Food |
| Salts | +1 Food, +5 pressure (+1 each to money/research/defense/energy/influence) |
| Syrup | +1 Food, +30% boost to influence pressure |

**Special Tier 0:**
| Resource | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| Iron | +4 Labor pressure, +30% support ship build speed |
| Coal | +2 Energy pressure, +30% Labor from civilian structures |
| Aluminium | +6 Labor pressure, +1 Pressure Capacity |
| Fulrate | +2 Money pressure, +4 native pressure |
| Pekelm | +3 Defense pressure, +100% colony ship speed |
| Morphic Materials | +2 pressure matching target planet's native type |
| Unobtanium | Morphs into any other resource you control |
| Drugs | Replaces 1 level requirement resource of any type |
| Spice | +3 Influence pressure, +6 planet loyalty |
| Rulite | +10 Energy pressure |
| Errite | +10 Research pressure |
| Irrite | +10 Influence pressure |
| Kallite | +10 Defense pressure |
| Phasite | +4 Research pressure, +1 research/s per 5 population |
| Allondium | +1 Influence pressure (not exportable), scaling bonuses by level |

#### Tier 1 — Requires Level 1 Planet

| Resource | Pressure |
|----------|----------|
| Textiles | +3 Money |
| Chemicals | +3 Research |
| Explosives | +3 Defense |
| Natural Gas | +3 Energy |
| Plastics | +3 Influence |
| Titanium | +8 Labor |
| Electronics | +3 Money |
| Luxury Materials | +3 Influence |
| Rare Metals | +3 Research |
| Hadocide | +5 Defense |
| Deuterium | +2 Defense, +2 Research |

#### Tier 2 — Requires Level 2 Planet

| Resource | Pressure |
|----------|----------|
| Neutronium | +7 Defense |
| Psionic Reagents | +7 Influence, no loyalty loss after 3 min |
| Supercarbons | +12 Labor |

#### Tier 3 — Requires Level 3 Planet

Rarest and most powerful. Typically unique or scalable resources with significant bonuses that increase with planet level.

### The Resource Network

Resources are not stockpiled quantities — they are **connections**. A planet with Water exports its Water to another planet. That receiving planet uses the Water (and other imports) to level up and generate pressure-driven income.

The economy is fundamentally a **directed graph**: planets are nodes, resource exports are edges.

## Star Ruler 2 — The Pressure System

This is SR2's most distinctive economic mechanic.

### How Pressure Works

1. Each imported resource provides **pressure** of a specific type (Money, Research, Defense, Energy, Influence, Labor)
2. Pressure drives **civilian auto-construction** of matching buildings:
   - Money pressure → Markets
   - Research pressure → Universities
   - Defense pressure → Defense buildings
   - Energy pressure → Power plants
   - Influence pressure → Influence buildings
   - Labor pressure → Factories
3. Each pressure point, if used optimally, yields a fixed income:
   - **1 Money pressure = 75k income** (if within pressure cap)
   - **1 Energy pressure = 0.5 Energy/second** (base, before modifiers)

### Pressure Capacity

Every planet has a **Pressure Capacity** determined by:
- Planet level (higher level = more capacity)
- Population size
- Special buildings (Metropolis adds +6 capacity)

**Under capacity:** Each pressure point builds one optimal building → full value.

**Over capacity:** Excess pressure builds suboptimal "dense" buildings → reduced value per pressure point. Normal buildings are 1:1 efficiency; dense versions are 1.5:2 (worse ratio).

**Out of tiles:** If the planet surface runs out of buildable hexes, remaining pressure builds even less efficient compressed buildings or is wasted entirely.

### Civilian Auto-Build

Players do **not** directly place most buildings. The civilian population automatically constructs:
- **Cities** — To house population (always built first)
- **Production buildings** — To match incoming pressure (type determined by pressure type)

Build rate is fixed per planet. Harsh biomes slow it down. Players can build special structures manually (see below), but most economic buildings are civilian-driven.

### Developed vs. Undeveloped Tiles

Planet surfaces use a **hex grid**. Tiles become "developed" as population grows. Buildings on developed tiles have lower construction and maintenance costs. Placing buildings on undeveloped tiles is more expensive but population will eventually develop the land beneath them.

## Star Ruler 2 — Planet Levels & Colony Management

### Planet Leveling

Planets level from 0 to 5 by importing required resources:

| Level | Requirements | Pressure Cap | Notes |
|-------|-------------|--------------|-------|
| 0 | (colonized) | Very low | ~1B pop, economic drain (-80k) |
| 1 | 1 Water + 1 Food | Low | Basic colony, ~+150k income |
| 2 | 1 Water + 2 Food + 1 Tier 1 resource | Medium | ~+500k income |
| 3 | 1 Water + 3 Food + 2 Tier 1 + 1 Tier 2 | High | Strong economy |
| 4 | 1 Water + 4 Food + 4 Tier 1 + 2 Tier 2 | Very high | Economic powerhouse |
| 5 | (escalated requirements) | Maximum | Empire centerpiece |

Each level grants:
- Higher population cap
- Higher base income
- Higher pressure capacity
- Ability to export higher-tier native resources

### The Pyramid Structure

The economy forms a **hierarchical pyramid**:
- Many Level 0 food/water planets (base of pyramid, individually small)
- Several Level 1 planets consuming food + water
- Fewer Level 2 planets consuming food + water + Tier 1 exports
- Very few Level 3-5 planets consuming everything below them

This creates natural **supply chain dependencies** — losing a food planet can cascade and de-level planets that depend on it.

### Colonization

Colonizing requires sending 1 billion population from a source planet. The source planet's population decreases accordingly.

### Homeworld

Your homeworld starts with 2 native resources (food + water), automatically making it Level 1. It serves as the initial hub of your resource pyramid.

### Player-Built Structures

While most buildings are civilian auto-built, players can manually construct:
- **Metropolis** — +6 Pressure Capacity (key for maximizing high-pressure planets)
- **Research facilities**
- **Military structures**
- **Orbitals** (stations in orbit):
  - Commerce Station — Connects disconnected trade zones
  - Various military/economic orbitals

### Surface Grid

Planets have a **hex grid surface** whose size depends on the planet's physical size. Larger planets have more tiles (up to ~200+), enabling more buildings and higher pressure utilization. Small planets may have fewer than 100 hexes.

Moon bases and Floating Continent artifacts can increase tile count.

## Star Ruler 2 — Logistics & Trade

### Territory-Based Trade

Resources can only be exported between planets in the **same territory** (connected trade zone, shown by colored borders on the galaxy map).

### Connecting Territories

Disconnected territories cannot trade. Solutions:
- **Commerce Stations** — Build one in each zone to enable cross-zone trade
- **Gates** — Permanent connections that also bridge trade zones
- **Colonizing within "Tradable Area"** — Gray border area around your territory; colonizing here extends your territory naturally

### Civilian Trade Ships

The civilian population physically ships resources along trade lanes in automated trade ships of varying sizes. This is cosmetic but has gameplay impact:
- If an enemy destroys a civilian trade ship, they gain its cargo value as budget income
- The planet gains a "Blockaded" status stack, reducing resource production
- This makes trade route protection strategically important

### Auto-Import vs. Manual

- **Auto-import** — Convenience mode; grabs the nearest available matching resource. Not optimal.
- **Manual management** — Player explicitly sets export targets. Better for optimization. Required when rerouting around captured planets or terraforming.

### No Galactic Bank

Unlike SR1, there is no shared resource pool. Each resource connection is a specific planet-to-planet link. This makes the network topology matter far more than in SR1.

## Star Ruler 2 — Budget & Global Resources

### Budget Cycle (3 minutes)

Every 3 minutes, a new budget cycle starts:
1. **Budget = Money Income - Maintenance**
2. You spend the budget on construction, fleets, etc.
3. Unspent money at cycle end goes to **social welfare** (research grants, housing, energy generation)
4. You can borrow against future budgets at significant penalty
5. Money does NOT accumulate across cycles (use it or lose it)

### Debt

If maintenance > income, you're in debt:
- Mild debt → reduced population growth
- Severe debt → fleet weapons stop firing

Debt is evaluated per-cycle, not cumulative — even empires with large reserves suffer if current maintenance exceeds current income.

### Global Resource Types

SR2 has five top-bar resources:

| Resource | Generation | Primary Use |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Money** | Budget cycles (from pressure-built Markets) | Construction, maintenance, everything |
| **Influence** | Fixed galaxy-wide pool: 6 × empire count, divided by influence stake share | Diplomacy cards, votes, treaties, annexation |
| **Energy** | From Energy pressure buildings (0.5/s per pressure) | Special abilities, FTL-adjacent powers |
| **FTL** | Slowly accumulated global resource | Faster-than-light travel (method-dependent) |
| **Research** | From Research pressure buildings | Technology progression |

### Influence — Zero-Sum Diplomacy Resource

Total galactic influence generation is **fixed at 6 × number of empires** and never increases. Your share is proportional to your influence stake vs. all other empires combined.

Example: If you have 30 stake and opponents total 20 stake, you get 60% of total galactic influence.

Used for a **card-based diplomacy system**: propose treaties, vote, attach riders, force votes to annex territory. Very unique mechanic.

### Defense Generation

Defense pressure generates free support ships distributed across your empire. Amount = same combined Labor cost in free supports, spread to planets/systems flagged to receive them.

### FTL Methods

| Method | Mechanic | Trade-off |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Hyperdrive | Ship-mounted jump drive, high speed | Flexible but FTL-expensive per ship |
| Slipstream | Temporary wormholes, anyone can use | Fast but visible and shared |
| Fling Beacons | Fire ships from beacons, 10s arrival | Infrastructure-dependent but cheap per use |
| Gates | Permanent instant connections, owner-only | High upkeep cost, caps on count |

## Star Ruler 2 — Economic Scaling

### Early Game (1-2 systems)

- Colonize food + water planets first (they're Level 0 drains: -80k each until fed to a Level 1+ planet)
- Get homeworld to Level 2 ASAP with food + water + Tier 1 resource
- Level 1 planets generate ~+150k, Level 2 ~+500k
- Food is the primary bottleneck
- Manual resource management is optimal

### Mid Game (5-15 systems)

- Build the resource pyramid: many food/water → some Tier 1 → few Tier 2
- Metropolis buildings on key planets to increase pressure capacity
- Influence stake becomes important for diplomacy
- Redirect food from self-sufficient planets (those that have grown their own) to new colonies
- Commerce Stations to connect distant territories

### Late Game (Galaxy-spanning)

- Level 4-5 planets are economic powerhouses, consuming entire supply chains
- Planet size matters enormously: Vast Plains/Ringworlds have enough tiles to fully utilize massive pressure
- Morphic Materials become extremely valuable (+2 pressure of native type, worth ~150k per attachment on money planets)
- Influence is zero-sum, so diplomatic power is about share, not absolute generation
- Auto-import becomes necessary for managing hundreds of connections
- High-tier special resources (Rulite, Errite, Irrite, Kallite at +10 pressure each) can single-handedly power planets

### Key Scaling Bottlenecks

1. **Food supply** — Every leveled planet needs food; food planets multiply fastest
2. **Pressure capacity** — Without Metropolis or large planets, pressure is wasted
3. **Planet tile count** — Small planets can't utilize high pressure regardless of capacity
4. **Territory connectivity** — Disconnected territories can't share resources without Commerce Stations/Gates
5. **Budget timing** — 3-minute cycles mean you can't save up indefinitely; timing large builds matters

## Key Differences: SR1 vs SR2

| Aspect | Star Ruler 1 | Star Ruler 2 |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Economic model** | Stockpile + production chain | Rate-based pressure + budget cycles |
| **Resource flow** | Galactic Bank (shared pool) | Direct planet-to-planet connections |
| **Construction** | Instant if resources available | Budget-limited per cycle |
| **Building control** | Player places all buildings | Civilians auto-build from pressure |
| **Production chain** | Ore → Metal → Electronics → Adv. Parts | No chain; resources are flat exports |
| **Resource depletion** | Ore depletes (but economy continues) | Resources are permanent (no depletion) |
| **Colony scaling** | More buildings = more output | Level system with tier requirements |
| **Trade mechanism** | Galactic Bank + Spaceports + Haulers | Territory-based export connections |
| **Money model** | Continuous accumulation | 3-minute budget cycles (use or lose) |
| **Diplomacy resource** | N/A | Influence (zero-sum, card-based) |
| **Player role** | Direct manager of production | Architect of resource network |

### Why SR2 Changed the Economy

SR1's production chain economy scaled poorly — at galaxy scale, managing individual factory ratios on hundreds of planets was tedious even with governors. The Galactic Bank abstracted away logistics to the point where geography didn't matter.

SR2's pressure system solves both problems:
1. **Reduced micromanagement** — Civilians handle building placement; player focuses on network topology
2. **Geography matters** — Territory-based trade and direct connections make location strategically important
3. **Emergent specialization** — The level/tier pyramid naturally creates planet roles without explicit specialization orders
4. **Meaningful trade disruption** — Destroying trade ships and cutting connections has real consequences (vs. SR1 where the Galactic Bank was mostly invulnerable)

## Open Source Codebase

### Star Ruler 1

**Not open source.** The code uses third-party libraries that can't be released. Only Star Ruler 2 was open-sourced.

### Star Ruler 2

**Repository:** [github.com/BlindMindStudios/StarRuler2-Source](https://github.com/BlindMindStudios/StarRuler2-Source)

**License:** MIT (source code), CC-BY-NC 2.0 (art assets)

**Community fork:** [github.com/OpenSRProject/OpenStarRuler](https://github.com/OpenSRProject/OpenStarRuler) — Maintained community version, multiplayer-compatible with commercial builds.

### Engine: StarFlare

Custom engine, fully available in the repository.

### Language Breakdown

| Language | Approximate Share | Role |
|----------|------------------|------|
| C | ~45% | Engine core |
| C++ | ~22.5% | Engine core |
| AngelScript (.as) | Significant | All gameplay logic, economy, AI, UI scripting |
| Flash | Minor | UI elements |

### Repository Structure

```
StarRuler2-Source/
├── source/           # C/C++ engine source (StarFlare)
│   └── msvc/         # Visual Studio solution (Windows)
├── scripts/          # AngelScript gameplay logic
│   ├── server/       # Server-side game mechanics
│   ├── client/       # Client-side rendering/UI
│   ├── shadow/       # Prediction/shadow scripts
│   └── definitions/  # Core definitions (version.as, etc.)
├── data/             # Data definition files
│   ├── resources/    # Resource definitions
│   ├── buildings/    # Building definitions
│   ├── subsystems/   # Ship subsystem definitions
│   └── ...           # Shipsets, biomes, etc.
├── locales/          # Localization files
└── (launcher exe)    # Just a launcher; real binary must be built
```

### Architecture Pattern: Data-Driven + Scripted

The engine (C/C++) handles:
- Rendering, physics, networking
- Core object systems
- Platform abstraction

AngelScript handles:
- All gameplay mechanics (economy, combat, diplomacy)
- AI behavior
- Resource/building/planet definitions
- UI logic

Data files define:
- Resource types and their properties
- Building types and costs
- Planet level requirements (planet_levels.txt)
- Subsystem stats and blueprints

This separation means the economy system is **entirely moddable** without touching C++ — you can redefine resources, pressure values, building effects, and planet levels by editing AngelScript and data files.

### Build Requirements

**Windows:** Visual Studio 2017, open `source/msvc/Star Ruler 2/` solution, build Debug or Non-Steam Release.

**Linux:** `make` from root. Dependencies: libpng, zlib, GLEW, GLU, freetype2, libvorbisfile, libvorbis, libogg, libopenal, libbz2, libXRandR, libcurl.

### Notable Details

- SteamWorks API code removed; platform abstraction layer kept
- All DLC checks bypassed; Wake of the Heralds content always available
- Music not included (copy from commercial install to `data/music/`)
- Multiplayer-compatible with commercial version
- `MP_VERSION` in `scripts/definitions/version.as` controls multiplayer compatibility

## Design Lessons & Patterns

### Relevant to Drift (a4x)

1. **Rate vs. Stockpile:** SR1 used stockpiles (instant build if resources available). SR2 used rates (budget per cycle). Drift currently uses rate-based modifiers (depotQuality, hardness multipliers) — closer to SR2's philosophy.

2. **Resource Tiers Create Natural Progression:** SR2's Tier 0/1/2/3 system creates a natural expansion incentive — you must colonize specific planets to access higher tiers. This maps well to Drift's multi-level survey system (survey depth revealing harder-to-access deposits).

3. **Pressure as Abstraction:** SR2's pressure system is an elegant way to handle colony building without micromanagement. Rather than placing individual factories, you define what a planet receives and civilians handle the rest. Could inform how Drift handles colony development.

4. **Territory-Based Trade vs. Magic Teleportation:** SR2's shift from Galactic Bank (SR1) to territory-based connections made geography matter. For a space sim, physical logistics (ship-carried resources) create more interesting gameplay than abstract resource pools.

5. **Pyramid Dependencies Create Vulnerability:** SR2's level system means losing a single food planet can cascade and de-level dependent planets. This creates meaningful strategic targets beyond just "the biggest planet."

6. **Budget Cycles Force Decisions:** SR2's 3-minute use-it-or-lose-it budget prevents infinite hoarding and forces players to make spending decisions regularly. Interesting alternative to continuous accumulation.

7. **Zero-Sum Diplomacy Resources:** SR2's influence system (fixed total, proportional distribution) prevents runaway leaders from dominating every axis simultaneously. Your diplomatic power is relative, not absolute.

8. **Data-Driven Economy:** SR2's entire economy is defined in data files and scripts, not compiled code. Resource types, pressure values, building effects, and planet level requirements are all moddable. Good architecture for iteration.
